About Me

Hi, my name is Ethan Karvelsson. I am a 3rd year Mechanical Engineering Student at Montana State University. I have a deep fascination with Aerospace and powered human flight. I am particularly interested in gas turbines and turbomachinery, such as jet engines, rocket engines, turbopumps, radial and axial compressors and turbines, etc.

I am originally from Houston Texas, where there is a large cultural impact from NASA's Johnson Space Center and Mission Control. Growing up, I often visited NASA's Johnson Space Center, where I got to see one of the only remaining entire Saturn V rockets in the US and in the world. I was also able to meet and talk to many cool employees and personel there.

I moved from Texas to the beautiful state of Montana at the start of highschool, where I have enjoyed many outdoor activities such as skiing, hiking, camping, and much more.

I have had a passion for tinkering and building things since a young age. I often experimented with electrical circuits and projects, chemistry experiments, and creating mechanisms and contraptions out of lego technics and other types of materials and resources.

I have always had a particularly keen interest in engines, especially when it pertained to car or aircraft. In high school I spent a lot of time working on cars and modifying and tuning them. I did not know exactly what I wanted to study in college until a few months after I graduated highscool. At that point I knew I wanted to study some kind of engineering, and I was torn between choosing Mechanical or Aerospace Engineering.

In October of 2024, I was staying in Houston Texas with my family, and I planned I spontaneous trip down to Boca Chica Texas, which is where SpaceX's original Starbase is located. I planned this spontaneous trip because on October 22nd, 2024, SpaceX conducted the 5th Integrated Flight Test of the Starship rocket program, and I did not want to miss the opurtunity to watch this historic event. So me and my family drove down to Boca Chica the evening before the launch window, and arrived in the wee-hours of the morning. When we arrived, we found a spot and waited until the launch. We then witnessed history as the SpaceX Starship 5th Integrated Flight Test rocket blasted off from the pad at 7:25 am, and the rocket slowly dissapeared into the sky. Then several minutes later, the booster fell back down out of the sky before it's engines fired, slowing it down to a stop right above the launch pad, where the "chopstick arms" caught it. Several seconds later the shockwaves from the booster coming down at supersonic speeds reached us as an earth shaking double boom. The double boom was caused by the bottom and the top of the booster creating their own distinct shockwaves. After witnessing this historic event, I knew I wanted to work in the Aerospace industry.

At some point in the fall of 2024, after doing a few weeks of research, I decided I wanted to pursue Mechanical Engineering at Montana State University, and I applied to start there in the spring of 2025. I decided I wanted to pursue Mechanical Engineering over Aerospace Engineering because I thought it would be more applicable for me since I want to work on gas turbines and turbomachinery.

I have now been studying at Montana State since January of 2025, and I am on track to graduate in with a Bachelor's Degree in Mechanical Engineering with a minor in Mechatronics in May of 2028.

I started working at World Wide Flight Services (WFS) at Bozeman International Airport (BZN), in May of 2025 as a Ramp Agent, working part time on the weekends while in school, and working full time in the summers. I am currently still working at WFS as a Ramp Lead now, where my day to day operations involve loading baggage according to load plans, verifying that the correct quantities of baggage are loaded into the correct cargo compartments, and sending baggage number and weigh information to the automated weight and balance systems (AWABS) of aircraft so the pilots have the AWABS data they need to take off. I also operate pushback operations where I drive the pushback tractor, pushing aircraft out of gates to prepare them to taxi to the runway. I work with many different types of aircraft, from Embraers ERJ's, Bombardier CRJ's, Airbus A320 series, Airbus A220's, Boeing 737-8/900's, and Boeing 757-200's.

For the past year and a half, I have also been building a DIY turbojet engine by using two BorgWarner turbochargers arranged in a compound or series configuration. If you are interested in learning more about this project please visit my projects page on my portfolio website, where I go into depth on the scope and the details invlolved in this project.
